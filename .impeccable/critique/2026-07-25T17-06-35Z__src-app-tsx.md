## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Authored for this product at the *content* layer, generic at the *visual* layer. The first-person hero, the real sprinting→injury→osteopathy narrative, real tiered GBP pricing, the specific "inside the Jon W Sports Therapy rooms" address, and the "amateur & professional athletes" line are things you could only write about *this* practitioner — well above the bar for a solo-practice page. But the visual system (Cormorant + Jost, calm teal #2f7d8a, off-white ground, pill buttons, soft shadows, one radial hero glow) is the default "premium wellness/spa" palette shared by a hundred massage sites. Verdict: specific brief, under-committed art direction.

## Overall Impression

The content is the star and it's genuinely good — this reads like a real person, not a clinic brand. The single biggest opportunity (and risk) is the Contact form: it silently sends nothing and shows a developer's TODO note as the "success" message, which can poison all the trust the hero and About build. Fix that and add real proof (reviews + GOsC number), and this is a strong little site.

## What's Working

1. **Content-level authenticity.** Hero + About narrative + real tiered pricing is exactly what most solo-practitioner sites fail at. It reads like a person.
2. **Robust card layout for uneven data.** `Services.tsx` handles the price / no-price asymmetry cleanly — `margin: auto 0 …` on both `.services__tiers` and `.services__price-note` keeps the "Book Me" buttons baseline-aligned across all three cards in the auto-fit grid, with zero JS.
3. **Above-average accessibility baseline.** Skip link, `:focus-visible` ring, labeled inputs with autocomplete, `aria-expanded`/`aria-label` on the toggle, titled map iframe, and `prefers-reduced-motion` neutralizing all reveals.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The contact form silently sends nothing — and says so.** `Contact.tsx` `handleSubmit` only calls `setSubmitted(true)`; there is no network call, and the success copy visibly includes "connect a mail service to deliver submissions automatically." On a health site the form is a booking channel: patients believe they've reached out, Matthew never receives it, and the confirmation exposes unfinished-dev text. *Fix:* wire a real endpoint (Formspree/EmailJS/serverless) with an error state; remove the dev note from user-facing copy immediately regardless; add a "send another / edit" affordance.

**[P1] The persistent "Book Me" disappears on mobile.** `index.css` hides `.nav__cta` at `max-width: 860px`, so on phones the primary conversion action only reappears after tapping the hamburger. On a Persuade surface where mobile is the majority, that's the most important action hidden behind an extra tap. *Fix:* keep a compact Book button in the mobile top bar, or add a sticky bottom booking bar on mobile.

**[P1] No social proof and no verifiable credentials.** Zero testimonials/reviews; "Registered Osteopath" has no GOsC number or link; no clinic photography beyond the portrait slot. UK patients specifically look for GOsC registration and Google reviews. *Fix:* add a short testimonials strip, surface the GOsC registration number (ideally linked to the register) near the trust chips, reserve a real clinic photo.

**[P2] No phone number anywhere; the "phone consultation" is email-only.** Contact offers email, a form, and a mailto for a *phone* consult — but no `tel:` link. Many osteopathy patients (often older, in pain) want to call, and you're making them email to book a phone call. *Fix:* add a `tel:` link in nav/contact/footer and let the free-consult CTA offer a direct call.

**[P2] Art direction doesn't match the brand promise.** Spa-calm teal + serif vs. copy that sells sports-science rigor. It under-differentiates from generic massage templates and mutes Matthew's distinct athletic/rehab credibility. *Fix:* introduce one confident on-brand device — a more athletic accent, a movement/anatomical motif, motion-forward hero, or a stronger typographic voice.

**[P3] Borderline inline-link contrast on the tinted ground.** `--primary #2f7d8a` on `--bg #f7f9fa` computes ≈4.4:1 — just under WCAG AA 4.5:1 for normal-size body links (e.g. the footnote links). *Fix:* use `--primary-dark #24616b` for inline body links on `--bg`/`--bg-alt`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (nervous first-timer):** No "what happens on your first visit" / what to bring / does it hurt; no testimonials to borrow confidence from; "Registered Osteopath" reads as claim not proof; three different "book"-labeled actions in Contact create paralysis at the anxious moment.

**Casey (distracted mobile):** Primary "Book Me" hidden on mobile; all key content starts `opacity:0` via `.reveal`/IntersectionObserver, so if JS is throttled a distracted user could see blank regions; no persistent conversion anchor on scroll.

**Sam (accessibility):** Good baseline, but the mobile toggle has no `aria-controls` pointing to `.nav__mobile`; the form has no `aria-live`/inline error region and the misleading "success" is announced as fact via `role="status"`; borderline-AA link contrast affects low-vision users.

## Minor Observations

- **Dead CSS:** `.hero__panel-art`, `.btn--light`, `.form__check` have no matching markup — remove or wire up. Related: the health contact form collects name/email/message with **no privacy/consent tick** — a GDPR gap worth closing.
- **Hero photo crop:** `/matthew-knight.png` is ~951×1024 into a taller `object-fit: cover` panel — the supplied photo gets cropped top/bottom; brief the client to frame with headroom.
- **Logo fallback:** `nav__logo` has real alt text so a missing `/logo.png` degrades to readable text, but there's no *styled* wordmark fallback — brand looks unstyled if the image 404s.
- **"Book Me" copy** was flagged by the reviewer as informal for a clinical site — but this was your explicit request, so it stays unless you decide otherwise.
- **Service images** at `aspect-ratio: 4/3` cover are robust to whatever the client supplies. Good.

## Questions to Consider

- If the free 15-minute phone consult is the lowest-risk on-ramp, why is it buried in Contact instead of sitting next to the hero CTA?
- Three booking pathways (bookingbase, contact form, mailto consult) — which one do you actually want people to use, and does the page make that unmistakable?
- The copy sells "evidence-based sports science." What single visual decision would make a returning athlete *feel* that in the first second?
- If JavaScript fails, how much of this page still works? Right now: reveal-hidden content may never appear and the form silently does nothing.
